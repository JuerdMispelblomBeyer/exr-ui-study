# Editorial turbine app — floating callout presentation

## Reference identity and evidence quality

- Source: `reference/presentation-only/pres-001__mobile-turbine-app__dark-editorial-presentation.jpg`.
- Type: presentation/marketing composition, not a direct application screenshot.
- Evidence: high for art direction and callout composition; low for desktop UI architecture and real interaction.

## Executive design reading

This is valuable as presentation language: angled product renders float through
large dark space while compact numbered callouts explain a few signature
features. It should influence documentation, onboarding, and focused detail—not
be mistaken for an operational dashboard.

## Dimension analysis

### TP-AES-01 — Aesthetic and personality

**OBSERVATION** — A nearly black vertical canvas stages glossy phone renders and
small dark callout tiles with acid-yellow markers.

**INTERPRETATION** — The work feels premium because it edits aggressively and
uses scale contrast, not because the UI itself is proven exceptional.

**TRANSFERABLE PRINCIPLE** — Use this level of selectivity when explaining one
platform capability, fault path, or subsystem story.

### TP-COMP-01 — Composition, grid, and alignment

**OBSERVATION** — Devices sit at angles and alternate across the canvas; dotted
connectors tie small feature cards to them.

**INTERPRETATION** — The composition creates guided narrative movement rather
than a reusable application grid.

**TRANSFERABLE PRINCIPLE** — For onboarding and empty/detail states, connect a
small number of explanatory lenses directly to the system object they describe.

### TP-DEN-01 — Density and whitespace

**OBSERVATION** — Large dark gaps separate each feature moment.

**INTERPRETATION** — Whitespace provides cinematic pacing but sacrifices
information throughput.

**TRANSFERABLE PRINCIPLE** — Apply generous spacing to documentation and
consequential flows, not to monitoring overviews.

### TP-TYPE-01 — Typography and hierarchy

**OBSERVATION** — A large two-line headline leads; tiny metadata and numbered
feature labels create editorial contrast.

**INTERPRETATION** — Type establishes a story hierarchy independent of cards.

**TRANSFERABLE PRINCIPLE** — Use large typography for a single explanatory
thesis and compact labels for evidence callouts; never enlarge generic greetings.

### TP-COLOR-01 — Color and contrast

**OBSERVATION** — Acid yellow appears only in icons, small underlines, and active
instrument details.

**INTERPRETATION** — Accent scarcity makes yellow feel like live energy.

**TRANSFERABLE PRINCIPLE** — Preserve ExR yellow as the active probe/current
signal and pair it with neutral materials.

### TP-GEO-01 — Geometry, borders, shadows, and surfaces

**OBSERVATION** — Dark rounded callouts float subtly above the background;
devices provide stronger physical depth.

**INTERPRETATION** — Elevation is assigned to annotation and product object,
while the canvas stays flat.

**TRANSFERABLE PRINCIPLE** — Let temporary inspectors float above a low-relief
system model; avoid turning every persistent region into a floating tile.

### TP-IMG-01 — Imagery and iconography

**OBSERVATION** — Photoreal phone renders dominate and are supported by small
yellow feature icons.

**INTERPRETATION** — The composition sells a product object; it does not show a
space-efficient desktop workflow.

**TRANSFERABLE PRINCIPLE** — In ExR presentation contexts, use accurate robot or
subsystem renders; in-app, prefer live diagrams and camera evidence.

### TP-HIER-01 — Information hierarchy, interaction, and motion

**OBSERVATION** — Numbered cards imply a reading sequence and dotted lines imply
association; static evidence does not prove animation.

**INTERPRETATION** — A guided sequence reduces cognitive load by revealing a few
claims at a time.

**TRANSFERABLE PRINCIPLE** — Use numbered, attached explanation steps for
onboarding or incident review, with no invented parallax or device motion.

### TP-DIST-01 — Distinctive decisions and limits

**OBSERVATION** — Angled product objects, enormous negative space, and tiny
luminous callouts define the style.

**INTERPRETATION** — These moves are excellent art direction for storytelling
but would harm routine operational density if copied literally.

**TRANSFERABLE PRINCIPLE** — Borrow the attached-callout grammar and accent
discipline, not the device-mockup staging.

## Signature moves

| Move | Why it works | Transfer | Do not copy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Floating numbered lens | Explains without obscuring object | Attached subsystem explanation | Exact card styling |
| Dotted association line | Makes the referent explicit | Connect inspector to selected node | Decorative connector |
| Accent micro-signal | Yellow remains potent | Current/probe/primary action | Acid palette values |
| Angled hero object | Creates cinematic depth | Accurate robot render in presentation | Phone mockups in app |
| Vertical story pacing | Makes features memorable | Onboarding/incident narrative | Monitoring layout |

## Translation decisions

- **Borrow:** attached callouts, yellow scarcity, editorial scale contrast,
  presentation pacing, elevation reserved for annotation.
- **Ignore:** phone mockups, turbine marketing content, vertical poster layout.
- **Brand-specific:** device renders, source logo/iconography, exact acid yellow.
- **Accidental:** visible device angle and shadows are presentation staging.
- **Anti-patterns/limitations:** not a direct UI reference; wastes operational
  space; tiny captions; dark-on-dark callouts; floating tiles can detach from
  causality if connectors are merely decorative.
