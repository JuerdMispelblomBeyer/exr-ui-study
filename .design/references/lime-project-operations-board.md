# Lime project operations board

## Reference identity and evidence quality

- Source: `reference/30459706c8e995d01bcaaac3c148af09.jpg`.
- Type: direct UI composition / modular project dashboard.
- Evidence: high for layout and component hierarchy; low for scrolling and motion.

## Executive design reading

The board creates energy through asymmetric modular sizing and a very controlled
high-visibility accent. A large project identity panel and roadmap anchor the
composition; tiny date, efficiency, time, and assistant modules orbit them.

## Dimension analysis

### PROJ-AES-01 — Overall aesthetic and personality

**OBSERVATION** — A sage canvas holds white, black, grey, and lime modules with
large type and selective portraits.

**INTERPRETATION** — The board feels optimistic and editorial because visual
weight changes across modules instead of repeating a dashboard template.

**TRANSFERABLE PRINCIPLE** — Use expressive massing and one concentrated signal
color to give operational surfaces personality without decoration everywhere.

### PROJ-COMP-01 — Composition, grid, alignment

**OBSERVATION** — Two large modules anchor the left; smaller modules form a
stepped cluster on the right, all aligned to a modular grid.

**INTERPRETATION** — Asymmetry encodes importance while clean shared edges prevent
the mosaic from feeling random.

**TRANSFERABLE PRINCIPLE** — In mixed-span workbenches, define one or two anchors
and let support modules occupy residual space on shared axes.

### PROJ-DEN-01 — Density and whitespace

**OBSERVATION** — The roadmap is information-dense; project identity and date
modules are sparse; gutters remain visibly equal.

**INTERPRETATION** — Density rhythm creates fast scanning and protects the focal
anchors from module noise.

**TRANSFERABLE PRINCIPLE** — Alternate sparse status/orientation tiles with dense
evidence tiles, rather than applying identical content density.

### PROJ-TYPE-01 — Typography and type hierarchy

**OBSERVATION** — Large compact sans headings occupy whole corners; numbers and
metadata use smaller neutral roles.

**INTERPRETATION** — Typography establishes module identity before icons or
borders and gives the board editorial confidence.

**TRANSFERABLE PRINCIPLE** — Let major task/state labels function as compositional
masses; keep machine metadata compact and aligned.

### PROJ-COLOR-01 — Color and contrast

**OBSERVATION** — Lime marks the primary project, active date, progress bars,
arrow, and chart line; black creates counterpoints.

**INTERPRETATION** — The accent forms a deliberate route across otherwise calm
neutral surfaces. In greyscale, mass and type still maintain order.

**TRANSFERABLE PRINCIPLE** — Allocate the lead accent across current scope,
current datum, and primary action so it behaves as one system signal.

### PROJ-GEO-01 — Geometry, borders, shadows, surfaces

**OBSERVATION** — Broad rounded modules use minimal shadow; inner timelines,
charts, pills, and circular badges switch geometry by function.

**INTERPRETATION** — Low-relief surfaces keep the composition graphic while
inner geometry supplies affordance and precision.

**TRANSFERABLE PRINCIPLE** — Keep persistent work surfaces flat and reserve more
specific shapes for controls, status, and data.

### PROJ-IMG-01 — Imagery and iconography

**OBSERVATION** — Small portraits appear only where ownership or assistance is
relevant; icons are sparse and bold.

**INTERPRETATION** — Imagery provides context rather than visual filler.

**TRANSFERABLE PRINCIPLE** — Use real assets only where they explain ownership,
physical state, or evidence; omit generic portrait decoration in engineering UI.

### PROJ-HIER-01 — Information hierarchy, interaction, motion

**OBSERVATION** — Progress, date selection, add-task, report selector, and AI
assistant affordances are visible; motion is unsupported.

**INTERPRETATION** — Each interaction remains inside its owning module, keeping
the board scan stable.

**TRANSFERABLE PRINCIPLE** — Attach controls to the instrument they affect and
avoid a detached global toolbar for local actions.

### PROJ-DIST-01 — Distinctive decisions and limits

**OBSERVATION** — Lime module, black counterweights, sculpted date tile, and
unequal spans repeat as a graphic grammar.

**INTERPRETATION** — Distinction comes from controlled contrast and composition,
though the bento format is trend-sensitive.

**TRANSFERABLE PRINCIPLE** — Use mixed spans only when size expresses diagnostic
importance; combine with a product-specific instrument to avoid generic bento.

## Translation decisions

- **Borrow:** accent scan path, mixed density, two-anchor composition, low-relief
  surfaces, and attached actions.
- **Ignore:** project-management IA, avatar placement, AI assistant tile, and
  exact modular proportions.
- **Brand-specific:** lime-on-sage palette and portrait treatment.
- **Accidental:** isolated composition lacks full shell and responsive evidence.
- **Anti-patterns/limitations:** bento can become a collection of unrelated
  rounded boxes; oversized sparse tiles waste space in routine operations;
  accent overuse would collapse semantic status.
