# System performance — spatial map workbench

## Reference identity and evidence quality

- Source: `reference/335837d927f5a30e3e8f61107c4833d8.jpg`.
- Visible brand: Splunk.
- Type: direct dashboard screenshot / spatial monitoring concept.
- Evidence: medium because of resolution; high for macro composition.

## Executive design reading

The reference organizes a complex monitoring surface around one map-like data
field. Trends, filters, counters, and alert history attach to its edges, so the
screen reads as one spatial instrument rather than a grid of independent cards.

## Dimension analysis

### MAP-AES-01 — Overall aesthetic and personality

**OBSERVATION** — A luminous granular map fills the center while small technical
instruments and alert markers occupy its perimeter.

**INTERPRETATION** — The system feels alive and consequential because activity is
shown as a spatial field, not reduced to totals.

**TRANSFERABLE PRINCIPLE** — Let the system's real topology or physical structure
become the primary visual object in diagnostic interfaces.

### MAP-COMP-01 — Composition, grid, alignment

**OBSERVATION** — A wide spatial core is framed by a slim left evidence rail,
right gauges, bottom event timeline, and attached filters.

**INTERPRETATION** — Supporting regions preserve the center's context and share
edges with it; no equal-card grid competes.

**TRANSFERABLE PRINCIPLE** — Compose supporting metrics as attached rails around
one topology or schematic, with alignment revealing ownership.

### MAP-DEN-01 — Density and whitespace

**OBSERVATION** — Fine-grained marks are dense where activity occurs; dark map
regions and gutters remain quiet.

**INTERPRETATION** — Density itself communicates system activity, while quiet
space makes anomalies visible.

**TRANSFERABLE PRINCIPLE** — Allow evidence density to reflect real system
activity rather than normalize every region to equal visual weight.

### MAP-TYPE-01 — Typography and type hierarchy

**OBSERVATION** — A compact sans labels tools and trends; large numeric counters
occupy small right-side instruments.

**INTERPRETATION** — Typography stays subordinate to the spatial visualization
but gives rapid numeric confirmation.

**TRANSFERABLE PRINCIPLE** — In topology-first views, keep labels concise and let
selected values become locally large without introducing a large page header.

### MAP-COLOR-01 — Color and contrast

**OBSERVATION** — Teal describes the continuous field; lime, orange, red, and
white mark distinct states and selected evidence.

**INTERPRETATION** — Color encodes activity and severity, though some meanings
appear color-dependent. Greyscale preserves macro hierarchy but loses categories.

**TRANSFERABLE PRINCIPLE** — Separate continuous system structure from discrete
status signals; pair status hues with symbols and labels.

### MAP-GEO-01 — Geometry, borders, shadows, surfaces

**OBSERVATION** — Thin rails, small circular probes, and translucent overlays sit
on a continuous dark field with little card elevation.

**INTERPRETATION** — Lightweight chrome preserves the map's spatial continuity.

**TRANSFERABLE PRINCIPLE** — Treat inspectors and controls as attached overlays
on a stable topology; elevate selection, not the whole surface.

### MAP-IMG-01 — Imagery and iconography

**OBSERVATION** — The map is rendered from data-like points; alert triangles,
pins, radial gauges, and timeline marks use a consistent technical language.

**INTERPRETATION** — Imagery is operational evidence, and icons mark status or
location rather than decorate.

**TRANSFERABLE PRINCIPLE** — Use schematic or physical imagery whose marks can be
selected, filtered, and tied to evidence.

### MAP-HIER-01 — Information hierarchy, interaction, motion

**OBSERVATION** — Event pins, selected labels, map controls, filters, and history
markers imply pan/zoom, selection, filtering, and time inspection; motion is not shown.

**INTERPRETATION** — Context-first drill-down is strongly implied. The map stays
visible while evidence appears around or on top of it.

**TRANSFERABLE PRINCIPLE** — Preserve topology context during selection and make
detail appear spatially attached to the affected node or link.

### MAP-DIST-01 — Distinctive decisions and limits

**OBSERVATION** — The point-cloud world, perimeter evidence rails, and bottom
event field form a recognizable operational silhouette.

**INTERPRETATION** — Product data—not generic cards—creates memorability.

**TRANSFERABLE PRINCIPLE** — Build visual identity from the topology and failure
modes unique to the target system.

## Signature moves

| Move | Observation | Interpretation | Transfer | Do not copy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spatial system field | Map owns most of viewport | Relationships precede totals | Make robot topology the dominant surface | World-map imagery |
| Perimeter evidence rails | Metrics attach to map edges | Context and detail coexist | Place summaries/filters around schematic | Exact rail positions |
| Activity-through-density | Point concentration shows activity | Density becomes data | Vary node/trace density truthfully | Decorative particles |
| Attached alert markers | Warnings live on their location | Cause is immediate | Put degraded state on affected subsystem | Source icons/colors |
| History under topology | Event band shares width with map | Time explains spatial state | Align event evidence under system diagram | Exact timeline |

## Translation decisions

- **Borrow:** topology-first composition, attached inspectors, density as data,
  perimeter evidence, and context-preserving drill-down.
- **Ignore:** global geography, security-product labels, exact point treatment,
  and wide-screen proportions.
- **Brand-specific:** Splunk identity, teal terrain treatment, and icon set.
- **Accidental:** low-resolution screenshot and unreadable microcopy.
- **Anti-patterns/limitations:** aesthetic particle fields can become decorative;
  high density needs zoom/filter states; color-coded incidents require redundant
  cues; small peripheral metrics risk illegibility.
