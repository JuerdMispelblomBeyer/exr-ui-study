# Distinctive patterns and signature moves

A signature move is a characteristic design relationship that materially
contributes to recognition. It must be more specific than a common component or
trend and more reusable than a proprietary brand asset.

## Signature threshold

A candidate move should satisfy at least three:

- visible without reading labels;
- recurs or organizes a major region;
- changes hierarchy, composition, interaction, or surface ownership;
- expresses the source's personality;
- survives removal of brand color/logo;
- difficult to replace with a default component without changing character;
- can be abstracted into a principle without tracing the source.

Do not count generic rounded cards, standard sidebars, ordinary tab bars,
unmodified line charts, or common glass/gradient effects unless their use forms
an unusual system-level relationship.

## Signature-move record

Identify 5–10 per reference using:

```markdown
### [Descriptive, non-brand name]
Supporting IDs: [reference observation IDs]

OBSERVATION
[Visible anatomy, location, recurrence, and relationship.]

INTERPRETATION
[Why it is characteristic and what effect it creates. Confidence: high/medium/low.]

TRANSFERABLE PRINCIPLE
[How to recreate the effect through another product's content and system.]

Dependencies: [content, interaction, layout, imagery, or system conditions]
Do not copy: [exact shape, asset, layout, text, color, or proportion]
Disposition: borrow | translate | presentation-only | reject
```

Name moves by their mechanism—`attached diagnostic lens`, `typographic anchor
against floating product stage`, `continuous signal path`—not by the source
brand.

## Four required classifications

### Principles worth borrowing

Keep effects and decision logic that can serve another product. State the target
condition that makes each principle useful.

### Implementation details worth ignoring

List exact proportions, source breakpoints, content quantities, component
anatomy, platform conventions, and technical artifacts that should not survive
translation.

### Brand-specific details

List logos, proprietary fonts, color combinations, illustrations, photography,
hardware, named motifs, and recognizable asset treatments. Explain which more
abstract effect may still be learned from them.

### Accidental details

List crop, compression, browser/device chrome, capture perspective, transient
content, inconsistent demo states, and production artifacts. Do not turn them
into intentional system rules.

## Anti-pattern and anti-copy audit

Flag:

- copied source information architecture;
- signature shapes or assets with labels/colors swapped;
- generic trend features mistaken for the reference's DNA;
- marketing-only spaciousness moved into high-density tools;
- decorative charts, topology, or controls without source functionality;
- accessibility or clarity sacrifices that work only in a staged image;
- interaction assumptions inferred from a still;
- exact palette/type recipes disconnected from the target brand;
- combining every signature move at once.

The downstream goal is a small signature grammar, not a collage of source
features.
