# Geometry, borders, shadows, and surfaces

Analyze geometry as a language of ownership, behavior, and material depth—not a
catalog of rounded rectangles.

## Shape language

Inspect:

- outer silhouette versus inner component shapes;
- angular, soft, organic, technical, or hybrid tendencies;
- radius ladder and where corners become exact;
- pills, circles, tabs, brackets, notches, cut-outs, and connector paths;
- cropping, masking, and continuation beyond a boundary;
- whether shapes signal enclosure, action, status, identity, or decoration.

A useful interpretation links a shape to its role. “Cards have 24 px corners”
is an implementation estimate; “the chassis is soft while instruments remain
exact, separating approachable shell from technical content” is design
intelligence.

## Border treatment

Classify edges:

- absent, hairline, heavy, double, inset, luminous, partial, or bracketed;
- high/low contrast;
- continuous or interrupted;
- semantic, structural, interactive, or decorative;
- consistent across surfaces or varied by ownership.

Determine whether borders do the work that surface contrast, spacing, or depth
should do. Meaningless borders are an anti-pattern, not a transferable motif.

## Shadow treatment

Record apparent softness, spread, direction, opacity, color, and layering only
qualitatively. Ask what the shadow communicates:

- persistent elevation;
- temporary/floating state;
- selected focus;
- ambient spatial staging;
- separation from imagery;
- or decorative “premium” polish.

Do not infer exact shadow values. Transfer an elevation rule, not a CSS recipe.

## Surface hierarchy

Map visible layers from back to front:

1. canvas/environment;
2. application chassis or persistent work surface;
3. nested instrument/content field;
4. selected object or active state;
5. floating inspector, tooltip, or temporary control;
6. modal or system overlay.

For each, note fill value, opacity, border, shadow, blur, overlap, and clipping.
Interpret how ownership and persistence are communicated.

## Geometry transfer matrix

| Source evidence | Possible principle | Do not automatically copy |
|---|---|---|
| Soft outer shell, exact inner plots | Contrast enclosure comfort with instrumental precision | Exact radius values |
| Floating panel attached to a selected object | Use depth to express temporary ownership | Panel position and source component anatomy |
| Partial borders/brackets around focus | Mark scope without enclosing the entire field | Proprietary bracket motif |
| Inset inner contour | Create material depth with restrained edge layers | Exact stroke colors/effects |
| Edge-to-edge continuous surface | Preserve relationships instead of card fragmentation | Source information architecture |

## Required analysis blocks

Create observation/interpretation/transfer blocks for shape language, borders,
shadows, and surface hierarchy. Explicitly flag universal pills, one rounded
box per concept, equal elevation, decorative glass, and meaningless strokes as
anti-patterns when visible.
