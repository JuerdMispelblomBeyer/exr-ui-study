# Information hierarchy

Analyze the sequence in which meaning becomes available. Do not equate
hierarchy with heading size or visual contrast alone.

## Five-second read

Record the first three things understood without zooming:

1. dominant object, message, or state;
2. supporting context or evidence;
3. expected action, continuation, or detail.

Then identify important information that remains intentionally quiet. A strong
reference often succeeds because it withholds visual weight from secondary
content, not because everything is highly styled.

## Hierarchy channels

For each level, identify the channels doing the work:

- scale and occupied area;
- position and sequence;
- light/dark or color contrast;
- type size, weight, width, and case;
- density and detail;
- enclosure and boundaries;
- depth, overlap, and blur;
- imagery crop and focal subject;
- motion or implied continuation;
- repetition versus singularity.

Interpret combinations. A singular image may dominate through crop and scale
while remaining low contrast; a small status may dominate through isolation
and saturated color.

## Hierarchy tests

### Greyscale test

Does the intended scan order remain when hue is removed? If not, color is
carrying structure and the transfer principle must include a non-color channel.

### Blur/squint test

Which masses remain? If many equal boxes survive, the reference may have a flat
category hierarchy despite polished detail.

### Task-order test

Infer the likely purpose from visible content, with confidence. Does the visual
order match the likely user decision, or is it optimized for marketing impact?
Presentation hierarchy may be valuable but must be classified accordingly.

### Removal test

Mentally remove the dominant element. Does the remaining structure still guide
the user, or does the design depend entirely on one hero treatment? This reveals
whether the hierarchy is systemic or staged.

## Transfer patterns

Useful principles are relational:

- one focal object earns most area while supporting evidence forms a quiet path;
- current state is attached to the affected object rather than repeated globally;
- sparse orientation precedes dense evidence;
- selection creates depth and local contrast without raising the whole screen;
- typography, not containers, establishes the top two levels;
- repeated elements remain quiet so a singular exception is unmistakable.

Do not transfer the source's information architecture if the target user's task
requires another order.

## Required analysis block

State the current scan sequence, the channels creating it, the likely reason it
works, and the transferable ordering rule. Label marketing/storytelling
hierarchy separately from operational hierarchy.
