# Imagery and iconography

Analyze what images and icons do in the composition, how they relate to content,
and which aspects belong to the source brand. Do not mistake a device mockup or
case-study render for interface structure.

## First classify the visual asset

For each major visual, label it:

- product/UI screenshot;
- device or hardware mockup;
- photograph;
- 3D render;
- illustration;
- diagram/topology/chart;
- texture/material field;
- icon/pictogram;
- decorative shape or light effect.

Record whether it is source content, brand presentation, or a compositing frame.

## Imagery function

Determine whether imagery is:

- primary evidence;
- explanatory or instructional;
- atmospheric/emotional;
- product proof;
- navigational;
- a spatial anchor;
- or decorative filler.

Then inspect crop, scale, perspective, masking, background removal, depth,
captioning, overlap, color grade, and relationship to type. Explain why the
treatment changes attention or meaning.

## Screenshot/mockup separation

When UI appears inside a phone, laptop, robot display, or browser frame, analyze
two layers separately:

1. **interface evidence** visible inside the screen;
2. **presentation evidence** created by perspective, hardware, lighting,
   background, callouts, and staged composition.

Presentation may teach depth, storytelling, or focal staging. It must not be
converted into UI padding, navigation, or surface hierarchy.

## Iconography analysis

Inspect:

- outline, filled, duotone, glyph, diagrammatic, or illustrative style;
- apparent stroke weight and corner character;
- optical size and container use;
- semantic clarity and label dependence;
- consistency across navigation, actions, status, and diagrams;
- custom/product-specific symbols versus library icons;
- whether icons carry interaction, category, status, or decoration.

Do not recommend copying proprietary icons. Transfer rules such as consistent
optical weight, role-specific containers, or custom symbols for product-specific
objects.

## Brand-specific and accidental detail

Treat logos, proprietary illustrations, product renders, signature photography,
mascots, device models, and recognizable icon systems as brand-specific unless
reuse rights are explicit. Treat compression artifacts, reflections, dust,
browser chrome, notification badges, crop errors, and temporary data as
accidental unless the composition deliberately relies on them.

## Required analysis blocks

Create observation/interpretation/transfer blocks for imagery role, integration
with composition/type, and iconography. Add a separate note describing which
pixels are presentation-only and should not influence the target interface.
