# Multi-reference synthesis

Synthesis creates a new visual thesis from several bodies of evidence. It is
not voting, averaging, or assembling one feature from each source.

## Normalize before comparing

For each source, reduce observations to mechanisms and effects:

- `large heading` becomes `typographic mass owns first read`;
- `floating cards` becomes `temporary layers attach detail to a stable stage`;
- `yellow highlight` becomes `one concentrated accent marks current state`;
- `dark background` becomes `low-luminance canvas isolates luminous evidence`.

This prevents brand color, exact layout, or fashionable components from
dominating the synthesis.

## Cross-reference matrix

Create:

| Principle/mechanism | Supporting references | Product value | Compatibility | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | common / complementary / conflicting | retain / combine / context-split / reject |

Trace each row to specific observation IDs or headings. Do not claim a common
principle because several references share an adjective.

## Six required synthesis views

### Common principles

Mechanisms present across multiple references. Explain whether they are truly
shared or merely common industry patterns. Ubiquity does not make a principle
distinctive.

### Complementary principles

Mechanisms that solve different layers of the same product: one may govern
composition, another typography, another interaction. Confirm they can coexist
without competing for the same visual channel.

### Conflicting principles

Name the conflict precisely: dense versus ceremonial, flat versus volumetric,
typography-led versus imagery-led, strict grid versus spatial overlap, quiet
accent versus expressive palette. Resolve it through target context, mode, or
priority—not compromise values halfway.

### Interesting combinations

Propose combinations where the interaction of principles creates something new.
State why the combination is compatible and what must remain restrained.

### Principles to avoid

Reject patterns that are copied, brand-owned, inaccessible, misleading,
presentation-only, overused, or incompatible with the target's content and
design system.

### Opportunities for a new visual language

Identify target-specific objects, relationships, states, or imagery that none
of the references use. These gaps are opportunities for original composition
and signature moves.

## Avoid the visual average

Do not resolve conflict by averaging radius, density, color, or spacing. Choose
a dominant principle for each layer:

- product/composition;
- hierarchy/density;
- typography;
- geometry/surfaces;
- color;
- imagery;
- interaction/motion.

One source may influence a layer, but no source should determine the entire
direction. Target product truth and existing design-system constraints arbitrate.

## ORIGINAL DESIGN DIRECTION template

```markdown
# ORIGINAL DESIGN DIRECTION — [working name]

## Target context
User, job, environment, content pressure, and existing-system constraints.

## Visual thesis
One sentence describing the product-specific formal idea. Do not name references.

## Intended personality
Three calibrated qualities and three explicit opposites.

## Dominant composition
Primary object, silhouette, grid logic, scan order, density rhythm, and whitespace ownership.

## Signature grammar
Two to four original moves, each with product meaning and permitted uses.

## Typography
Role contrast and compositional function; no unlicensed source fonts.

## Color
Role allocation, accent budget, contrast logic, and brand-system relationship.

## Geometry and surfaces
Shape roles, edge grammar, elevation, and persistent/temporary ownership.

## Imagery and iconography
Asset role, crop/integration rules, and source-independent icon language.

## Interaction and motion
State, disclosure, continuity, and motion purposes—not copied transitions.

## Explicit exclusions
Copied layouts/assets, incompatible principles, generic trends, and accidental details.

## Reference traceability
For internal use, map each principle to supporting observation IDs without describing the target as looking like a source.

## Open design decisions
Questions that require product content, system inspection, or composition exploration.
```

## Originality tests

Before handoff:

1. **Source-hidden test:** the direction is understandable with reference names
   and images removed.
2. **Product-substitution test:** replacing target content with another SaaS
   product should break the composition.
3. **Structure-distance test:** the target information architecture and dominant
   mass do not reproduce one source.
4. **Signature-budget test:** two to four meaningful moves dominate; the design
   is not a collage of all extracted motifs.
5. **System-fit test:** the direction can reuse or deliberately extend the
   target's typography, color, spacing, and components.
6. **Anti-copy test:** brand-specific and accidental details are absent.

If any test fails, revise the synthesis before sending it to a designer.
