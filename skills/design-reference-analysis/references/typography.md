# Typography and type hierarchy

Analyze typography as a compositional and information system. Do not identify a
typeface from appearance alone or treat font choice as the whole analysis.

## Role inventory

Map visible roles:

- display or narrative headline;
- screen/context title;
- section/object heading;
- body or explanatory copy;
- control label;
- operational value and unit;
- caption, metadata, identifier, code, or timestamp;
- state, warning, or annotation.

Record which roles are absent. A reference may intentionally collapse roles to
feel quiet or expand them to create editorial drama.

## Analyze the hierarchy mechanism

For each role, inspect:

- relative size and visual area;
- weight and width;
- line height and paragraph rhythm;
- case and tracking;
- color/contrast;
- alignment and proximity;
- serif, sans, mono, display, or humanist category;
- numerals, units, punctuation, and tabular behavior;
- relationship to imagery or UI objects.

The interpretation must explain what wins and why. Example: a headline may
become the primary compositional object because its line breaks create a large
asymmetric mass, not merely because it is “large.”

## Tests

### Type-only silhouette

Ignore imagery and containers. Does the type mass still establish focus,
rhythm, and structure? If so, typography is compositional. If not, it may be a
supporting labeling system.

### Contrast ladder

Order roles from strongest to weakest using size, weight, contrast, and
position. Determine whether the ladder is `compressed`, `moderate`, or `steep`,
and whether that supports the reference's purpose.

### Content-pressure check

Look for long lines, narrow wraps, data alignment, truncation, and realistic
labels. A beautiful placeholder headline may not transfer to operational copy.

## Transfer guidance

Borrow relationships such as:

- typography carries the first read before imagery;
- a compact mono layer separates machine metadata from human explanation;
- a restrained body face allows one expressive display role to dominate;
- line breaks create deliberate compositional width;
- values and units use contrast and alignment for rapid comparison.

Do not borrow exact typefaces, proprietary font treatments, copy, line breaks,
or scale ratios unless the target content and licensing support them.

## Required analysis blocks

Produce separate observation/interpretation/transfer blocks for:

1. overall typographic character;
2. hierarchy and role contrast;
3. typography's compositional function;
4. data/numeric or metadata treatment where present;
5. implementation or brand-specific details to ignore.

Invalid conclusion: “Use a bold sans-serif headline.”

Useful principle: “Concentrate typographic contrast in one narrative or state
role, then keep operational labels compact and quiet so the first read remains
stable even when content density increases.”
